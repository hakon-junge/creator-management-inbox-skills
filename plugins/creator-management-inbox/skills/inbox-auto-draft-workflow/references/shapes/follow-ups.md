# Follow-ups - the re-engagement ladder

Which follow-up a quiet thread gets is `follow-up-ladder` (`shapes/scheduling.md`); this
file holds the ladder for a money offer we sent. A Block 3 thread: the tempo rules stay in
the playbook core.

**The re-engagement ladder (counter out, thread gone quiet):**

1. **Around day six:** one no-pressure line ("hope my note reached you - inboxes being what
   they are"). No restating the offer (`restate-ask`).

2. **Between rounds - the appreciation touch (situational, any point where both sides
   retreated to think):** one email, ZERO ask - pure acknowledgment of the work they put into
   their proposal ("whatever we land on, I want to say I really appreciate how much thought
   you put into building this"). Emotional compensation is worth multiples of dollar
   compensation; a counterpart who feels appreciated mid-stall measurably softens their next
   counter. Not a bump - a thank-you.

3. **Around day fourteen: "Have you given up on [the specific thing]?"** - "have you given up
   on getting this locked for September?" Sent **word-for-word, nothing added** - in email
   it's the SUBJECT LINE and a near-empty body; every extra sentence dilutes it, and watering
   it down ("just wondering if maybe...") kills it. It works because no feels safe to say, and
   giving up is a loss - the two strongest triggers in one line. (Voss. Never use it as a cold
   opener - out of context it's the manipulation tell in `defensive-tells.md`.)

4. **Read the response - the decoder:**
   - **Instant re-engagement or deal (roughly 20-30%).** It was buried in their chaos, not
     dead. Move immediately - you have their full attention for one reply.
   - **"Yes / we've moved on" (roughly 20%).** You just saved weeks of polite silence. Close
     warmly, log the real reason in the creator's note, keep the pipeline door open.
   - **Anything in between = the nurture bucket.** They're close but something unspoken is in
     the way: summarize their position until "that's right" (they need to feel heard before
     they'll say what's stuck), then "what's stopping us from making this work?"
   - **Dead silence = the answer is yes, they've given up** - and they couldn't say it. Move
     on; don't send another. One exception worth a further move: a bigger-org or manager
     contact going silent often means they LOST INTERNAL INFLUENCE and are embarrassed to say
     so - drop the business thread entirely and offer a no-agenda human touchpoint; they'll
     tell you what happened as long as you don't ask.

5. **Genuinely done:** the breakup email as the graceful final close ("Bad timing? Not the
   right fit? Or worth revisiting in a few months? Either way, closing the loop from my
   side"), then stop. The pipeline keeps them; don't chase.
