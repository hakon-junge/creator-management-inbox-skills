# Approval and go-live shapes

How the approval and the go-live instruction get written; the review itself and the
feedback shape: `shapes/content-feedback`.

## Approval (video / final content)

The five beats, in order:

1. **Warm one-liner answering THEIR note first.** Praise is specific only when someone
   actually watched; unwatched content (`unseen-content`) gets generic warmth, never
   invented visual specifics. Never re-praise what the last sent message already
   complimented - recycled specifics show nobody read the thread.
2. **Approval early and unambiguous.** Burying the approval under three paragraphs of
   logistics makes a creator read the whole email anxiously.
3. **Go-live instruction.** Where the link and code go, plus the tracking link as a
   **plain URL on its own line** (copy-paste type - they have to select it), with where it
   leads ("the feature page where your community can learn more").
4. **The form step**, hyperlinked, if they are on the form process - with the gate
   mechanic explained (approve the draft, which unlocks the form for the live link), not
   just the instruction. People comply with mechanics they understand.
5. **Short excited close.**

**The tracking link goes IN this email, never deferred.** Hunt the thread history and
campaign emails for THIS video's link. Genuinely unfindable → a loud
`[TRACKING LINK - paste before sending]` and flag it as THE fill-in for the report. A
creator who publishes while waiting for a link posts untracked, and that is unrecoverable.

No payment-timeline line by default.

**Bundle all go-live blockers here** as short warm paragraphs - a date shift, a thumbnail
or logo fix ("One small ask -"), a form gotcha. The one job is getting this video live,
and every blocker that arrives in a separate email is another day of delay.

### Veteran trim

A creator several videos in gets NO description/code/CTA boilerplate: approval + form
line + warm close. State a code or link only when it is genuinely new information - their
first video, a changed code, a specific campaign link. New creators and first videos get
the full instruction.

**Campaign-tied videos override the trim.** Any video made for a live launch campaign gets
THAT campaign's tracking link and promo code in the approval, even for a veteran, even for
an agency-represented one.

*Worked example (fictional):* Tomas Lind is agency-represented and eight videos in - full
veteran trim territory. But this long-form is tied to the current feature launch, so the
approval still carries that campaign's tracking link and campaign code. **The tracking
link is never silently dropped under "veteran trim."** Include it, or flag
`[TRACKING LINK - paste before sending]`. Trim the boilerplate CTA prose, never the
campaign link or code itself.

And a great video still earns big specific enthusiasm up front. The trim is about
logistics boilerplate, never about warmth.

### Launch campaigns

- **A shared campaign code is auto-active.** Write "the [CODE] code will be active
  automatically", never "I'll send you the code" - only the per-creator tracking link gets
  sent. Promising to send something that does not need sending creates a loop you then
  have to close.
- **Posting after the offer window closes** → proactively tell them not to mention the
  expired code or offer. They will otherwise publish a video pointing at a dead offer.
- **Multiple language cuts = ONE form submission**, tied to the primary version.
- **Keep one video's logistics clustered.** A second video's items go at the END, never
  interleaved. Interleaved logistics for two videos is how a creator applies video A's
  code to video B.

### Expired launch offer

Fall back to the creator's **evergreen personal code** (`{{CREATOR_PROMO_OFFER}}`),
matched to the video's subject. **Never cross another live campaign's offer onto a
different feature's video** - the landing page will not match what they are talking about.
Name the replacement code and paste the link now, in this email.

## Live + submitted

Never a silent skip. A creator who publishes and hears nothing assumes it fell through a
crack.

1. "Saw it go live 🙌"
2. The submission landed, nothing else needed from them.
3. **Routine payment reassurance** - the flow is triggered on our side. No amounts and no
   terms. Only include this if submitting actually triggers payment on their process;
   soften the tense if it has not kicked off yet. A soft arrival window is fine once
   payment is confirmed-triggered ("should land this week"). The no-dates rule is about
   not committing amounts or terms - it is not about withholding a reassuring ETA from
   someone waiting on money.
4. Warm close. Add "just added it to the tracker" on the acknowledgement.

**One job only.** No next-video reminder, no renewal talk - especially not for a creator
flagged cut in the portfolio.

**Invoice attached.** Some creators attach an invoice PDF alongside the live link.
Acknowledge it *inside the same beat*. Do not treat it as a separate finance action and do
not CC finance: "Got your invoice too, and everything's logged on our side ✅ Payment's all
set in motion, so you should see the `{{PAYOUT_VENDOR}}` notification come through to
accept it this week 🙌"

**Read the live page** and drop the real view count into the go-live beat when it is
landing well: "wow, 11K views already in just a few days 🔥". Specific and true beats warm
and generic, every time.

## An asset the creator submits for something WE control and curate

*(The forward-looking-close principle below is an always-on close rule and also lives in
the compass; the carve-out is the intent-specific case kept here.)*

Use a forward-looking close **only when there is a real, agreed, in-progress next step.**
Cut it when the next thing is pending or unconfirmed.

**For an asset a creator submits for something we control and curate** - a landing-page
testimonial, a feature on our own page - **do not promise to share the finished page back
and do not guarantee inclusion.** The team may still adjust or curate. Close on "I'll let
you know in case our team needs to slightly adjust anything before we go live", not "I'll
add you and share it once it's live."

The difference matters because the second version is a promise made on behalf of a team
that has not agreed to it.
