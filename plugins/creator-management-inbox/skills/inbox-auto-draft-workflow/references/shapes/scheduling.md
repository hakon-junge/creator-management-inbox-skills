# Scheduling, reschedule and follow-up shapes

## Scheduling / timing

Warm and low-pressure. If sooner genuinely helps, say so with a reason and give an easy
out: "I'll be transparent - sooner would really help us on the campaign side."

Ground every date in the go-live dates on record: the thread and our sent history, the
creator's note (`inbox creator <name>`), the CRM `data-sources.md` names. Cross-check a
creator-stated date against the record and flag mismatches - "as planned, the 14th" when
the record says the 20th is a misalignment to catch now, not on the 20th.

Offer **two concrete options** and let them pick. An open "when works for you?" produces a
week of silence; two dates produce an answer.

Close the tracker loop out loud (`done-claims` if it isn't updated yet).

## Reschedule (creator moving a COMMITTED go-live date)

**This is not a "no worries" thread. Absolution trains chronic delay.**

The distinction that makes this shape work: "No worries at all, life gets busy :)" is the
right response to a late *reply*, or to a genuine one-off. It is the wrong response to a
moved commitment, because it tells the creator the date was never really a date.

**(a) Acknowledge, don't absolve.** "Thanks for the heads up, I appreciate you flagging it
ahead of time."

**(b) Match the response to the pattern.**

- A routine one-off with a sensible creator-given window → accept the window and note it
  in the tracker.
- A repeat or compounding slip → pin a specific day, put the ownership on them, and frame
  it as lining things up around their video: "Which specific day works for you? Once you
  confirm, I'll get everything lined up around it." Never "I'll hold you to it."

**(c) Name the pattern once, "we"-framed.** "Since we've had to shift this one already,
let's settle on a date we can both work to this time." Once. Naming it twice becomes
nagging and the creator stops flagging slips in advance, which is strictly worse than the
slips.

**(d) The always-on lever: anchor the date to a REAL benefit to them.** An expiring offer
window means a weaker CTA and less in it for them. Launch momentum means more reach.
**Don't manufacture a benefit if none exists** - a fake urgency lever is detectable and
costs more than the delay.

**(e) Chronic delayers get one light forward-expectation line**, partnership-framed.

No guilt, no passive-aggression. The firmness lives in the concrete ask and the real
reason, never in the tone.

## Follow-up bump (our message unanswered ~5 days)

<!-- rule:follow-up-ladder -->
**Which follow-up, keyed by who owes what:**

- **A non-money ask** (an approval, a date, a form, an answer): one bump around day 5,
  then stop. No second bump; re-open later only with new information.
- **Our money offer is out:** the re-engagement ladder (`shapes/follow-ups.md`) - a nudge
  around day 6, "have you given up on...?" around day 14, then the breakup email. A Block
  3 thread.
- **A contracted deliverable is late:** not a bump - `shapes/late-deliverables.md` (the
  creator moving the date themselves: the Reschedule shape above).

The creator has most likely forgotten they even opened the email. Re-surface it warmly
rather than waiting longer - waiting longer does not make them more likely to reply, it
just makes the thread colder.

The bump shape:

1. "Just bumping this back up in case it got buried (happens to the best of us haha)"
2. <!-- rule:restate-ask -->
   **One line restating the ask on the table** (the approval, the date, the question), so
   they don't have to scroll. This is the part people skip and it is the part that gets
   the reply. **Never restate a money offer** - in any follow-up, on any thread: it reads
   as anxiety and invites a counter.
3. An easy out.
4. A low-pressure close: "No pressure either way - just let me know what you're thinking
   :)"

**Never guilt-trip or imply they're slow.**

## Concrete time anchors

**The single most common edit on forward commitments: vagueness replaced with a date.**

"Well ahead of time" / "as soon as things are locked" / "the moment you confirm" all get
replaced with "next week". Pull the real date from the campaign timeline. **A knowable
date is never hedged.**

Future deliverables get a PROPOSED specific date with a light escape hatch: "maybe like
October 10th then? we can also lock the date later".

Vague forward commitments read as drafted by a machine. Dated ones read as written by the
person whose name is on the email.
