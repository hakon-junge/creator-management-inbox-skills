# The Voss toolkit (how you make the math land emotionally)

Negotiation is emotional, not a logic contest. Mental models, NOT scripts: wording comes
from your comms style; the examples only illustrate, never paste them. Load this for live
pushback on our number or a complex multi-party thread (SKILL.md keeps a short summary).

## The five essentials
- **Tactical empathy** - their perspective before your constraint, the spine of every
  reply. Disclosing our own bad news (a negative ROAS) is empathy that builds trust.
- **Accusation audit** - say the worst they might think before they do ("I know that's not
  the number you asked for"; "I fear this is way too far off for you"). The highest-value
  tool; pairs with the "no benchmark yet" line. Don't pull punches - a watered-down audit
  reads as fear: biggest accusations first, three or four at a time, let them sink in,
  then invite the rest out ("and there's probably something here I haven't even named").
  Disarmed negatives let positives surface - often they argue YOUR side.
- **Labels** - "It seems like...", "It sounds like...", then stop and leave the silence;
  defuses a negative, grows a positive. Never "I think..." - the label is about THEM. Late
  in the day or with a tired counterpart, a label ("seems like the monthly fee is the
  piece that matters most to you") beats a what/how question: it costs them no thinking.
- **No-oriented questions** - "would it be unreasonable if...", "is it too early to...":
  inviting a "no" makes creators feel safe and in control. Flip yes-questions: not "does
  this timeline work?" but "is there any part of this timeline that doesn't work for you?"
  (Case: "would it be unreasonable to lock in..." closed two pieces at the held rate.) The
  strongest, "have you given up on...", is the re-engagement weapon with its own ladder
  (`shapes/follow-ups.md`).
- **Calibrated questions** - "what would make this work for you?", "how am I supposed to
  do that?" (no heat); one or two per email, never an interrogation. When they push, ask
  "what would make this work for you?" instead of conceding a number. Attested first-offer
  pairing: the basis of your number + one curious question about how near it sits to what
  they had in mind. **The 2-in-10 read:** "how am I supposed to do that?"
  draws anger -> they're cornered and the offer may be near their true best: cool off,
  reassess it as a possible ceiling, and bail them out if you can (lasting loyalty).
- **"That's right", never "you're right"** - summarize their worldview (paraphrase +
  label) until they'd confirm it, then counter; especially before recalibrating an
  established fee. Lay it on thick: if it doesn't feel overdone, it isn't enough. "You're
  right" from them is a non-adoption tell (they feel talked at): don't advance to the
  number yet. After a genuine "that's right": "so what's stopping us from making this
  work?"; on a stalling deal, "what would stop us from getting this done?"

## Deep cuts

- **The question behind the question** ("what makes you ask?", core rules). Ask WHAT
  (uncovers problems) and HOW (drives implementation) - both feel like asking advice. WHY
  is a universal accusation trigger: "what made you go with...", never "why did you...".
- **The second no** ("your offer is very generous - it just doesn't work for us") kills
  external criteria (full play in `value-levers.md`). Let no out a little at a time,
  warning it's coming.
- **Mirroring** - in email only SOFT mirrors (their word inside a fuller question); bare
  one-to-three-word mirrors read as sarcasm in writing (fine on calls: `call-mode.md`).
- **Ackerman, adapted** - never open at a lowball (it breaks the fairness anchor). The
  rate-engine emits the ladder on every card (OPEN -> two roughly halving steps -> TARGET,
  to the nearest ten, never above TARGET/p75): use its steps; this file governs what
  happens between them - empathy each round, limited authority once (`limited-authority`),
  an end-sweetener, the anchor on the mechanism range. Precise numbers on contested
  threads ($1,940, not $2,000) read as computed, which they are; round ones are fine for
  friendly, uncontested packages - go precise when the number must HOLD.
- **Counter by re-scoping** - an out-of-range rate gets no match, split or accepted
  downsell: change the deliverable to what serves US and price THAT (Scenario R in
  `plays.md`: a long-form ask plus a short-form downsell became "two organic integrations
  into your regular videos" at our per-unit rate). The same principle sizes non-cash
  contributions: a giveaway or sponsorship tier is deliberate (the pack names the tiers).
- **Bending reality** - deadlines are mostly artificial (hold your own "reply by" dates
  loosely; never let one force a bad deal). The positive fairness frame: "I want you to
  feel treated fairly the whole way - so stop me at any point if you ever feel I'm being
  unfair, and we'll sort it." Audit before naming a disappointing number. Frame inaction
  gently as a loss (the launch window, the early slot) - felt loss motivates, threatened
  loss reads as a threat - and never manufacture false scarcity.
- **Negotiator types**, read from the first substantive reply: Analyst (math + time; bonds
  in silence - let it sit), Accommodator (draw hidden objections out with no-questions;
  fills silence - let them), Assertive (be crisp, acknowledge fast; summarize them back
  before anything lands). Assertives reset fast, so hold lines with them - but respect
  before playfulness. Mapping: hope-forward vision-doc creators = accommodators
  (enthusiasm is connection, not agreement - pin every specific); dashboard-citing
  creators = analysts; agency managers and top-tier creators = assertives. Deception
  patterns: `defensive-tells.md`.
- **Black Swans** - every creator cares about something more than the fee (a competitor
  sponsor, a personal project, why a past deal soured); broad calibrated questions surface
  it. Hold the fee, satisfy them elsewhere: the engine of "trade, don't give".
- **Leverage types** - positive (what we give), negative (what they lose, gently),
  normative (their own stated values - most underused). Leverage is psychological
  (Posture).
- **Async is the 7% channel** - email strips tone and body language: compensate with
  explicit labels and audits, and read latency, message length and dropped topics (a
  shrinking reply is a signal). End the email ON the key question, nothing after the ask,
  and let the thread sit once a counter is out (see Cadence).
