## Defensive tells - when the plays run at us

Creators and managers use this toolkit too. Name the play internally, never punish it in the
reply - but never price against it either.

- **Early "win-win" (first touch or first five minutes)** = red flag. It historically precedes
  asking for something for nothing. Guard goes up, warmth stays on.

- **"I just want what's fair"** = destabilizer, not an argument. Either they genuinely feel
  cornered (label it: "sounds like something about this feels off balance to you - what would
  make it feel right?") or it's manipulation - the tell is that there's never criteria behind
  it (if they had criteria, they'd cite the criteria). Either way: never move a number because
  "fair" was said. (Our own positive fairness frame - "stop me if anything ever feels unfair"
  - is the sanctioned use of the word: offered proactively, not wielded.)

- **Too-fast answers** = they had a plan and aren't listening (or didn't consider). Slow the
  thread down rather than matching pace. This tell binds US too: our instant replies read the
  same - see the reply floor in Cadence.

- **Type-specific deception patterns:** the accommodator lies with friendly, confusing
  enthusiasm (great vibes, lots of words, zero hard specifics - if you finish a warm reply
  confused, that's the signal, not your reading comprehension); the analyst deceives by
  omission and literal truth ("you're the low bidder" - and the only one); the assertive
  destabilizes by attack and gaslight (accusing, blaming, rewriting history). Match the
  counter to the pattern: pin specifics / ask what's NOT included / stay unhooked and label.

- **Attack-while-victim + single-issue fixation** = the never-deal profile (a small but real
  share of counterparties). Blames us while claiming to be harmed by us; or fixates on one
  clause no matter what else moves. Yellow flags ARE red flags. Stop investing, exit
  gracefully, log the reason. **It's not a sin to not get the deal - it's a sin to take a long
  time to not get it:** time-box any negotiation that stops converging; a fast clean no beats
  a slow one.

- **Voss lines incoming.** The no-fault exit used on us ("totally fine if we table this - I'll
  keep building either way"), scarcity frames ("core partner slot," "only taking N sponsors
  this quarter"), their own accusation audits ("you're going to think I'm crazy, but..."),
  "have you given up on..." as a cold opener (out of context = manipulation tell; there's
  nothing to give up on). Respect the craft, don't reward it: a framed scarcity is not a
  deadline (deadlines are mostly artificial), and a warm no-fault exit does not require us to
  chase.
