# Deal architecture - define the destination before you price anything

Load for any money thread with more than one line or surface on the table (sequence step 1
in the playbook core).

The goal of a negotiation is not to reach agreement on their proposal. It is to lead the
conversation to the shape WE defined before reading their message - and ideally land better
than that. Their email is raw material, not the agenda. Before any counter, three calls:

1. **Our ideal shape, written first.** Surfaces, cadence, price, sequence - decided before
   their proposal frames you. If you read their itemized sheet first, their structure
   becomes the battlefield; if you write ours first, theirs becomes a parts bin.
2. **Placement potential + system role.** Score each proposed placement twice: standalone
   potential (rate-engine EV per deliverable) AND its role in the system (top-of-funnel
   feeder / capture / depth / retention). A placement that only makes sense "because the
   others exist" gets priced inside a package (`package-pricing`) or not at all - never as
   its own standalone line.
3. **Build order.** Start-small-and-add is the default: unproven surfaces enter as
   controlled tests, and every ADD is a reward for proven conversion - which keeps each
   future expansion a lever WE hold. Everything-at-once is reserved for packages where each
   component is individually proven, or a strategic established re-book (see Bundles below).

The execution moves that follow from architecture:

- **Shrink the negotiation surface.** Choose which lines EXIST, not just what they cost.
  Countering every line of a five-line proposal means defending five fronts; a reply that
  acknowledges two lines and is silent on the rest re-frames the whole deal. (Worked case:
  Sasha Bell pitched five lines; the reply carried one echo-accepted line plus one house
  number and nothing else.)
- **Echo-accept - their words become the contract.** When their proposal contains a line
  whose price-to-scope already favors us, accept THAT line by restating their own bullet
  near-verbatim ("exactly as you framed it"). The justification-sweeteners they bundled in
  to defend the price (extra platforms, email capture, Stories mentions) become locked scope
  at their own number. The mechanism here isn't our math - it's their words; there is
  nothing to counter-argue. This is also the defensive read on their ranges: a creator who
  writes "$600-$1000 depending on type" has offered us $600 - take the floor.
- **Micro-spec inside the echo.** While restating their bundle, tighten the deliverable
  slightly upward (a required brand mention in the top-of-funnel videos, a link placement, a
  minimum duration). Small, reasonable additions pass frictionlessly inside "their own"
  framing.
- **Silent omission beats argued rejection.** A line we won't buy is not declined - it is
  absent from the accepted shape. Arguing a line legitimizes it as negotiable; omission
  de-tables it. Their sunk investment in the omitted asset (a list they built, a funnel they
  run) keeps pulling them back toward the path we DO offer. Resist adding a graceful pointer to
  the omitted line; full silence is stronger. Expect them to reopen it once: one soft
  pointer to that path is the maximum response, never a price. (Worked
  case: Sasha Bell's monthly email-sequence line - never mentioned again by either side.)
  **The always-omit ask-classes:** future-rate locks and benchmarks ("same rate next time",
  "this sets my baseline"), guaranteed renewals / multi-quarter commitments,
  and anything probing our budget ceiling or internal economics. Answer every OTHER point in
  their list and let these be absent - engaging them at all constrains future pricing power.
  (An exclusivity ask escalates unless a module prices it.)
- **Ranges cut both ways.** Whoever receives a range takes the end that favors them. THEIRS:
  their floor is the working number. OURS: expect the creator to ask for our ceiling - so
  when quoting median-to-p75, pre-frame the top as the CAP, not the standard, in the same
  message (see Scenario A.1).
- **Reconcile the totals.** Itemized cadence x rate must equal the headline number before
  any money email goes out. A stated total that doesn't match the items is a question to
  resolve first, never a thing to draft around.

## Bundles (multi-channel packages) - earned, never an entry move

<!-- rule:package-pricing -->
Creators pitch multi-channel packages up front (long-form + short-form + newsletter +
stories, "I think you'll love it"). Park the package and run the focused test on the
primary channel first - bundling is EARNED by performance, not offered at the start. Once a
creator's main channel is proven to convert, folding their other surfaces around the winner
can genuinely make sense - and then the bundle is ONE package: **every deliverable carries
its own number, inside one bolded headline total** (items x rates = the total, per
"Reconcile the totals"). Never a lump sum with the parts unpriced, and never a part
"calibrated later" - the package reads cheaper because its total beats the parts bought
alone, not because the parts are hidden. If a bundle wouldn't actually be better for both
sides - unproven creator, or a "package" that isn't really cheaper - it's the wrong
structure: warm no, back to the test.

For the counter to a creator-pitched multi-LINE system (each surface priced as its own fee),
run the moves above (Scenario N): echo-accept the favorable line, house-number what we want
on our terms, silently omit the rest.
