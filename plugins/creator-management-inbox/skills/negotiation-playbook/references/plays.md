# Scenario plays - the lettered detail

Routed from the verdict and `above-band` routing in SKILL.md core. Pick the match; combine
plays when a thread hits more than one. Plays that belong to a program mechanic (D,
G.1-G.3, G.6-G.7, I, J, N) live in that module's file, loaded by `inbox program` when the
module is on. Cases below are fictional and one line each; real numbers come from
`rate-engine`.

- **A - Counter above anchor, gap bridgeable (routed by `above-band`).** Restate the
  mechanism, limited authority once, land at or just under p75, hold - don't split. On a
  new or not-yet-profitable creator, the instinct is the opposite of raising: see if scope
  (drop to two) or timing holds the fee. Module levers only at their pushback
  (`aff-entry`). No-fault exit + embedded onboarding link.

- **A.1 - They ask for the top of our quoted band.** Expected (the range rule in
  architecture), so pre-frame the cap when quoting: p75 is the CAP, not the standard, and
  accepting it on a first collab makes our ceiling the starting price and burns later
  upward room. Hold below p75 (mid-band is the target), explain cap vs standard inside the
  fairness frame ("p75 is where the mechanism tops out - your data supports being in the
  band, not at its ceiling on a first collab with no shared numbers yet"), and trade
  written commitments (value lever 6) for the hold instead of cash. (Case: a first collab
  held mid-band this way and closed the full series.)

- **B - We choose to move (strategic creator / strong fit).** Hold with scope, timing or
  value first; spend cash only when those are exhausted or the creator is strategic
  enough. Movement is CONDITIONAL ("if we commit to two, and if you allow the usage
  option, would you be open to $X?"), at or below the midpoint, never above p75, framed as
  hard-won (one authority reference), after acknowledging the legitimate part of their
  argument (production cost, channel economics).

- **C - Big gap, effectively a decline (routed by `above-band`).** Show the whole formula
  and let the math do the declining ("I fear this is way too far off for you"). Never
  critique their rate - it's just outside our mechanism. Then value levers to justify the
  number, then the active modules' fallback (never the opening olive branch). Door open,
  no chasing.

- **E - "What's the budget?" (they ask us to name first).** Answer within one business
  day - deals stall exactly here. Whether you first ask what they had in mind is
  `first-number`; either way don't stall: if they want a figure now, they get the range.
  With their data: the calculator range (median to p75) with the mechanism attached.
  Without it: ONE email that gives a directional range for their tier AND asks for the
  analytics that firm it up - never a naked "send me your stats first". Anchor toward the
  lower end for an unproven creator, paired with the first-deal scope. Number + scope,
  then stop: no value levers in this first answer (never spend a lever before they've
  countered). **Returning creator, split on prior performance:** fine -> open warm on the
  history, name the PRIOR RATE as the anchor and ask for current analytics in the same
  reply (flat holds, stronger nudges up, weaker recalibrates down); BAD (weak ROAS
  despite spend) -> never mention the old rate (evidence we overpaid) and never open with
  a number: current stats first, then anchor low as a fair re-exploration ("performance
  wasn't quite where we'd hoped, but there's a real opportunity to re-explore if you'd be
  open to recalibrating rates with us").

- **F - Inbound pitch from an existing / past creator.** Their pitch data is THEIR anchor:
  re-anchor on OUR data ("your median on the channel is 3.2K"). Weak past performance is
  disclosed as shared, explicitly no-fault context ("our ROAS is very negative based on
  the last two shorts - not your fault, just the reality"). Enthusiasm for the idea comes
  first, kept separate from the money. A signal that the rate feels low, or that the brand
  benefits more -> the transparency lever.

- **H - Their rate breaks the formula, basis unknown (routed by `above-band`).** Unlike C
  (a gap declined by math), you don't know their basis yet - so before countering or
  declining, ask the calibration question: how did you arrive at that, what are you
  pricing on (full play in `references/value-levers.md`). Engagement-quality signals
  (comment quality, an audience asking them for advice, real answers back) justify a
  premium; vibes ("I've built my following", "they're loyal to me") or "my last collab
  paid that" do not. A benchmark basis gets the second no ("generous - but it doesn't work
  on our side") before the mechanism. Vibes on a first collab = firm pushback, the one
  place we're comfortable to walk away - gracefully, via the sustainable / long-term
  frame, door open. **No-number variant:** in decline-by-math territory (multiples above
  the ceiling) name NO counter figure - offer the path ("a first test at a level closer to
  our mechanism") instead. (Case: asks at 7-11x the band; granting the case first,
  separating worth from number and framing the test as the source of shared data led to a
  self-deferral, closed on the pause-not-break exit.)

- **K - Ending a deal early for underperformance.** Growth renegotiations wait for the end
  of the deal. Wanting OUT is the real case, mostly on deals of more than two pieces (two
  or fewer: run the remaining piece through). No-fault, results-grounded, converting
  rather than killing where possible (the active modules name the conversions); it changes
  agreed terms, so decide it deliberately, performance data in hand, before anything
  reaches the creator. In every underperformance recap, BEFORE proposing a no-fee
  structure, ask **"what other surfaces do you own?"** (case: a sub-1K-reader newsletter
  found this way became a paid trial that beat the video deal); price a newly surfaced
  deliverable off `rate-engine`, never off the conversation. **Under water, and they want
  to continue:** we may PROPOSE the make-good - creator-initiated extra content rides as
  unpaid bonus inventory, the remaining paid slot moves to the highest-conversion moment.
  (Distinct from the unpriced-scope rule, which bans absorbing freebies on a HEALTHY
  deal.)

- **R - The re-scope counter (routed by `above-band`, ~1.3-2x MAX).** Autonomous - don't
  escalate a number that is merely high. You keep ownership of scope: **creators move the
  price, you move the deliverable.**
  1. **Stage-frame the limit, no apology:** "Totally get it, thanks for sharing, sadly
     it's a bit out of range for us for the first test collaboration with you." The
     ceiling is about the STAGE, not their worth - that is why it lands without offence.
  2. **Keep the door warm:** "I really see the potential in our partnership..."
  3. **Re-scope rather than match or split:** the format WE want at a number the bands
     support, with the data rationale ("more data points before scaling").
  4. **No module lever in this counter** - each enters on its own rule (`aff-entry`).
  5. **Double agency close:** "How does that sound?" on the offer, "What do you think?" at
     the end.

- **L - Upfront-payment ask.** Hold the standard-terms line warmly (`deals.md`, "Upfront
  payment"): payment runs through `{{PAYOUT_VENDOR}}` after each post goes live, and it's
  genuinely fast (`{{PAYMENT_SPEED_CLAIM}}`) - reassure with the speed, don't renegotiate
  the structure ("the payment flow runs through our payments provider on our side"). The
  fast-pay claim is usable only while operationally true.

- **M - Post-agreement obstacle (freeze the terms, move the container).** An obstacle
  after agreement (a sponsor-exclusivity conflict, a format that suddenly can't happen):
  restate every commercial term as UNCHANGED (fee, code, dates, every agreed term) and
  re-scope only the vehicle, with two concrete alternatives for the creator to pick from
  (one offer to the human still holds). The deal stays closed. (Case: a competing-sponsor
  conflict; fee, program terms and code pinned, the video moved to an adjacent angle.)

## G - End-of-deal renegotiation

Results-specific, the recalibration delivered as promised ("we said we'd revisit once we
had real data - here's where it lands"), any increase tied to the demonstrated numbers.

- **G.4 - Recalibrating an established creator DOWN (the full sequence).** Accept their
  history claim as TRUE and separate it from the number ("that kind of long-term brand
  impact is real, and I don't doubt it happened the way you describe"); state the data
  constraint without touching content quality; limited authority ONCE; change the
  DELIVERABLE, not only the price (a dedicated becomes an integration), so the new number
  attaches to a new thing; hand back two non-cash items. The order is what lands it (case:
  a ~50% cut accepted in fourteen minutes).
- **G.5 - Never open a renewal with an unprompted raise.** On a profitable renewal, name
  the renewed SCOPE; the first number follows `first-number` and is never a raise; uplift
  only as the TRADE for scope. Never-spend-a-lever applies to renewals exactly as to first
  offers (case: an unasked +10% was banked at once and countered above, plus a
  milestone-ladder ask on the top deliverable).
- **The three canonical pushback reasons:**
  1. **"Other brands pay more."** Slow the thread down; ask the calibration question (H) -
     niches, never names. A business-model gap gets the justify-back plus the close the
     budget module names (the consistency counterweight on an always-on program; first
     call on the next campaign on a campaign one).
  2. **"We've worked together so long."** Accept the longevity claim as TRUE ("that's
     right" first) and separate it from the number. The data was under-shared, so they
     assumed compounding that wasn't happening: radical transparency - real numbers inline
     (the bar, conversions), the macro shift, not-personal plus management-blame, and the
     standing commitment (value lever 3): every run now comes with a full breakdown plus
     creative suggestions, so the recalibration is provable, not asserted.
  3. **"My production costs are high (team, editors...)."** The softest touch: sincerely
     acknowledge the quality and the cost behind it ("and we do try to factor that into
     our rate"). Their cost structure is theirs; our rate prices the content's
     performance. Their true floor above our ceiling -> pause, don't break.
