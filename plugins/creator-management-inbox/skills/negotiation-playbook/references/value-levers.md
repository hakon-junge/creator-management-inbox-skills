# Value levers - the closing value stack (rate-pushback and big-gap plays)

Numbered so other files can point at them ("value lever 6"). The active pack and modules
add their own levers to this list.

These are VALUE levers - you're not giving anything away, you're surfacing real value the
collab already carries to justify the number. Hold them back until there's genuine pushback
or a real gap. Order once the money is contested:

1. **We pay fast.** `{{PAYMENT_SPEED_CLAIM}}`, against an industry standard of thirty days
   and sometimes sixty to ninety. For the small-business owners and creator-entrepreneurs we
   work with, that gap is real money. Hint at it, don't say "cash flow": "we also pay quickly
   - I hope that's genuinely useful on your end." Mid-to-late gap-closer, not an opener.
   **On a recalibration this is the double-push: a lower rate paid within days is worth more
   than a higher one on the terms most brands run - pair the held number with the payment
   reassurance in the same message.** Keep the creator-facing claim to exactly what is
   attested and currently true; never round it up. (Internal confidence, never written:
   consistent multi-year payment history is something a random one-off brand deal cannot
   offer, and its value compounds as the market tightens.)

2. **What they get beyond the fee** - the pack's creator offer (its PACK.md), framed as
   included and generous, never as a concession.

3. **Post-collab transparency (the long-term / trust lever).** After a collab, share the
   actual numbers - what converted, what didn't, a straight read from the brand side, real
   results in the program's success metric. Use it especially when a creator suspects the rate is low
   and that the brand benefits more: it's the proof we calibrate fairly. "We take these
   partnerships seriously and long-term - after we run this, I'll share the actual numbers
   with you, what converted and what didn't, and my straight read from our side. I want this
   to be a fair balance both ways, and the data is how I prove that." **Standing commitment:
   every run going forward gets a detailed performance breakdown PLUS creative suggestions on
   what's working - delivered via the performance-review recap.** On a recalibration this is
   the behavior change that travels WITH the new rate: historically the real numbers were
   under-shared, so creators assumed a compounding that wasn't happening. The negotiation
   email cites the key numbers inline (the bar, conversions - internal economics never) and
   commits to the full breakdown; the recap delivers it.

4. **Anchor to logic, not to us (the fairness frame)** - the core rule, said out loud when a
   rate is contested.

5. **Group / herd proof.** Because the method is fair and consistent, you can offer something
   most brands can't: talk to any creator we've worked with. They'll confirm three things - we
   pay fast, we're fair, and onboarding is light and flexible on your time (we trust you to
   make what works for your audience). Use it on a hesitant creator, especially a brand-new
   one weighing whether we're legit. The offer itself is the proof; they rarely take it up,
   but extending it signals confidence - and it comes from a position of wanting THEM.

6. **Written commitments.** When you can't move on cash and won't move on scope, formalize
   future upside in writing as the trade for holding the fee. Bullet-list them at the bottom
   of the hold email so they read as commitments, not vibes. Three that repeatedly work:
   (a) **recalibration standard** - what data future rates will be run against (e.g. a
   segmented median, not a blended one), (b) **how future rates move** - the specific
   performance metrics that trigger movement (the program's success metric, revenue), and
   whatever terms the active modules add. This is what closes a "hold
   at $X" when the creator has done their homework and is asking why they should accept less
   than the ceiling of your band. The written form is the concession; the numbers don't move.
   (Proven-with-close on Priya Raman's first collab.)

> **Operational-truth caveat (levers 1 and 5).** Only usable while actually true. If payments
> are slipping right now or onboarding is friction-heavy, don't lean on those levers - a
> creator can verify them (including by asking another creator), and a lever they can disprove
> is worse than no lever. When in doubt, don't assert it.

## When a rate breaks the formula - ask how they calibrate

Sometimes a creator names a rate so far above the band that it doesn't just sit at the ceiling
of the mechanism - it breaks the basis you'd normally anchor to. Before you counter, before
you decline, the default move is a single sincere, open question: **ask them how they arrived
at the number.** Three ways in, pick one:

- how are you calibrating your own rate?
- how did you get to the figure you're sharing?
- what mechanisms are you using to price it?

Frame it as genuine curiosity, never a challenge: we'd genuinely like to understand how you
value yourself, your channel, your content and your audience - partly so we can check whether
we're even in the same ballpark. This puts the ball back in their court and makes THEM show
their work, the same way our mechanism shows ours. (It's the direct-creator version of the
"how do you calibrate your fees?" flip we use on managers in Scenario I.)

**When their basis is a benchmark, use the second no.** If the justification is external
criteria - "brands like X pay $Y," "market rate for my size is $Z," "my last collab paid that"
- don't debate the benchmark (a criteria war has no winner; each side picks numbers they
like). The move: "that's a generous structure you've put together - it just doesn't work on
our side," THEN the mechanism. Calling it generous nurtures generosity; "doesn't work for us"
is internal and unarguable from outside; and it warns incrementally that a no may be coming,
so the no never blindsides. (Voss. Symmetric defense: when a creator says "that doesn't work
for me," we can't benchmark them either - go to the calibration question above.)

**Names are off-limits; niches are the question.** When their basis is "other brands pay
more," never ask WHICH brands - it invites an NDA breach and reads as calling a bluff. Ask
what TYPE of brand or NICHE those rates come from, and the answer justifies itself back: "that
makes sense for their business model - ours works on different margins." It converts their
benchmark into our business-model constraint, which is unarguable from outside - and
creators often volunteer enough on their own to verify against their channel. Close the loop
with what the budget module offers (the consistency counterweight, or first call on the next
campaign).

**What you're listening for.** Most often the answer is vibes: "I've built up my following,"
"they're loyal to me and to my content-creation process." Those are real to the creator, but
they don't map to anything we can rank or calibrate a rate against - they describe reach and
sentiment, not converting value. The signals that DO justify a premium are engagement-quality
signals:

- the quality of the comments on their content
- followers asking the creator specific questions for advice and input
- the creator actually giving valuable answers back - a genuine advisory relationship with the
  audience, not just eyeballs

**The red flags.** If the logic is all vibes - or worse, "my last collab just paid that" -
those are red-flag signals. On a first collab especially, push back firmly. This is the one
place where we are directionally comfortable to walk away.

**How to walk away - always gracefully.** Never critique their number or their worth.
Explain clearly and respectfully that all brands are different, and that we work in a way
that is deliberately sustainable and, we believe, quite creator-friendly: we price so that
we can find creators we can work with long-term, support them, and support their channel
growth - rather than do a one-off that's a higher payday in a short window but doesn't build
anything lasting for either side. Leave the door open (see the breakup email in
`shapes/follow-ups.md`). A graceful walk-away today stays reopenable later; a bruised one
does not.
