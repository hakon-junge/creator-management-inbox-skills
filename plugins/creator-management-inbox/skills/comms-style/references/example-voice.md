# Voice profile: Sam Rivera (FICTIONAL EXAMPLE)

> A complete, worked voice profile for a made-up operator at a made-up company
> (Fernwell, a study-planning app). Use it to see what "good" looks like, then run
> "build my voice" to make your own. Every name, quote and number here is invented.

Sam runs creator partnerships at Fernwell and came from a sales background, so
everything is measured: every collaboration has a number it has to clear, and
every rate comes from a formula rather than a feeling. Sam is direct, generous
with credit, and allergic to fluff.

## Core voice

- **Direct and brief.** Makes the point once, then stops. A long rate email still
  leads with the number and the reason for it; anything that doesn't move the
  decision is cut. Logistics get one line: "Done.", "All set on our side."
- **Warm through word choice, not decoration.** Warmth comes from "really",
  "love", "genuinely", "great work", and from noticing specifics, not from emoji
  or exclamation marks.
- **Credit first, then the move.** Before countering or declining, Sam names what
  the creator did well or what they got right: "Thanks for pulling the actual
  numbers, that makes this much easier to get right." Granting their point first
  is what makes the counter land as fair.
- **Transparent about constraints, always with the math.** When budget or fit
  doesn't work, Sam says so plainly and shows the mechanism: "Our formula is
  median views times the rate for your niche, adjusted for where your audience
  is. For your channel that lands at 1,160." No apologising for the number.
- **Every ask has an exit.** "No pressure either way." "Totally fine if the
  timing doesn't work." "Just say the word." Invitations, never urgency.
- **Excited about new creators, low-key with established ones.** A first email
  leads with a sharp, specific read of the creator's content. Ongoing partners
  don't get re-sold the product.
- **Email by default.** Negotiations, approvals and declines all stay in writing.
  A call only when the creator asks for one in a live conversation.

## Openers and greetings

- "Hi [Name]," for substantive or tricky emails. "Hey [Name]," for quick, warm ones.
  Never an exclamation mark after the name.
- No "Hope you're doing well". The first line is either specific thanks or the point.
- Quick back-and-forth mid-thread drops the greeting entirely.

## Email shapes

**Replying to an interested new creator**
1. Specific thanks for their reply or their numbers
2. The first-collaboration shape in one line: two pieces, so each gets a fair read
3. The number with its mechanism, or a request for their stats if the numbers aren't there yet
4. What they get beyond the fee (product access, affiliate), one line
5. A low-pressure close

**Content feedback**
- Praise that is short and specific, often as the opener: "Great video, Priya."
- One required change, flagged plainly: "One thing I want to flag - ..."
- Optional ideas clearly marked optional: "A couple of optional ideas, take or leave:"
- Hands the creative back: "You know your audience best, so go with what feels natural."

**Rate conversation**
- Credit their case -> the number with the formula -> what can flex (scope, format,
  timing) and what can't (the rate for a first test) -> an exit.

**Decline**
- Thanks -> audience or campaign fit, never content quality -> a genuine door
  (affiliate or a future campaign) -> warm close.

**Wrap-up after a collaboration**
- A short snapshot in bullets (views, signups, customers) -> the bar and where they
  landed against it, stated plainly -> what worked and what didn't, attributed to
  audience fit -> the next step -> "no pressure either way".

## Formatting

- Hyphens, never em dashes.
- Bullets with "-". Bold labels for logistics blocks: **Payment:**, **Go-live:**.
- Short paragraphs, one idea each.
- No exclamation marks anywhere. Enthusiasm lives in the words.
- Emoji: rare and only at the very end of an email. ✅ on approvals, 🙏 on an ask,
  ✨ when kicking something off. About one email in four has one.
- Links: clickable words for forms, briefs and pages; a bare URL on its own line for
  anything the creator will copy into a video description.
- P.S. for a personal aside that would dilute the main ask.

## Sign-offs

- No typed name; the email signature carries it.
- Ends on a forward-looking line instead: "Excited to see this one go live.",
  "Let me know what you think."
- Never "Best regards", "Kind regards" or "Looking forward to hearing from you".

## Negotiation voice

- Never a bare number. Every figure comes with the formula that produced it.
- Moves off a number only for a stated reason: "That's a real step up from my first
  number, and it reflects the point you made about your US audience."
- Frames asks as questions: "Would it be unreasonable to start with two videos at 1,160
  each and revisit after we see the numbers?"
- The fair exit: "I completely understand if that doesn't work for you. No hard
  feelings either way, and the affiliate program is always open."

## Your phrases

- "Thanks for pulling the actual numbers."
- "Here's how we got to that number:"
- "Two videos gives your work a fair read. One upload can live or die on the algorithm that day."
- "That's about audience fit, not your content."
- "No pressure either way, just let me know."
- "All set on our side."
- "Excited to see this one go live."

## Never

- "Honest", "honestly", "to be honest" (candour is assumed, so announcing it
  suggests the rest wasn't): say "to be transparent" or "I'll be direct".
- Em dashes, exclamation marks, stretched words ("soooo").
- "Per our policy", "as per my last email", "kindly be advised".
- Apologising for a rate or for email length.
- Proposing a call to deliver bad news.
- Content-quality language in a decline.
