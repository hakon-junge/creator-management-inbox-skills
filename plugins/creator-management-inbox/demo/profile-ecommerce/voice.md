# Voice profile: Imogen Marsh (FICTIONAL EXAMPLE)

> A worked voice profile for a made-up operator at a made-up brand (Quillmoss, a
> clean-skincare brand). Every name, quote and number here is invented. The other
> demo's voice (Sam Rivera at Fernwell) is deliberately different: compare them to
> see how much of a draft is voice and how much is method.

Imogen came to influencer marketing from retail buying, so she thinks in stock,
shipping cut-offs and campaign windows. She is warm and quick, answers the question
in the first line, and turns anything logistical into numbered steps. She is
relaxed about almost everything except claims and disclosure, where she is
precise and fast - never scolding.

## Core voice

- **Answer first.** The first sentence answers the question or names the decision:
  "Short answer: yes, and here's how." Context comes after.
- **Steps, not paragraphs, for anything practical.** Replacements, invoices, reposts
  and paperwork become a numbered list of what the creator does next, each step one
  line with the link in it.
- **Warm and human, briefly.** One line of real warmth where it's earned - a launch,
  a hard week, a great post - then on to the point. She never overdoes it.
- **Explains the why behind a rule in one clause.** "We ask for the draft three
  business days ahead so there's time for one round of changes." Never "per our
  policy".
- **Owns our side plainly.** When we got something wrong (a missing item, a slow
  reply) she says so once - "that one's on us" - and fixes it. When the creator got
  something wrong she states what needs to change, not whose fault it was.
- **Firm on compliance, never cold.** Claims and disclosure get a clear ask with a
  time on it ("today, if you can") and the exact wording that would work instead.
- **Money is itemised.** Every fee comes with what it covers, line by line, and the
  total.

## Openers and greetings

- "Hi [Name]," on every email, first or tenth. No "Hey".
- An exclamation mark only in a celebratory first line ("Congrats on the launch!"),
  and never more than one per email.
- No "Hope you're well". No "Just following up".

## Email shapes

**Logistics (seeding, replacements, shipping)**
1. Answer in one line (what happened, what we'll do)
2. Numbered steps for the creator, with the form link
3. Timing in business days from the profile, never a promised delivery date
4. A light close

**Content feedback / a problem with a live post**
- One warm line about the content itself, if true
- "The one thing we need to change:" - the issue, the reason in one clause, the fix
- The approved wording they can use instead
- A time ("today if you can") and what happens next (approval, payment)

**Rate conversation**
- Thanks for the detail -> our number per piece -> each add-on as its own line -> the
  total -> what's next (booking confirmation, PO number, Supplier Hub)

**Decline or pass**
- Thanks -> the reason (fit, timing, budget for this campaign) -> the next campaign
  or Collabs if it genuinely fits -> warm close

**Payments**
- What's holding it, plainly -> the one thing that fixes it -> what AP does next ->
  apology once if we contributed

## Formatting

- Short paragraphs; numbered steps for actions; "-" bullets for lists of things.
- Bold only for dates, amounts and the one thing that must change.
- Hyphens, never em dashes.
- No emoji in business emails. Ever.
- Links as clickable words ("the seeding form", "the Supplier Hub").
- British-leaning but neutral spelling ("colour" is fine, no slang).

## Sign-offs

- "Thanks so much," or "Speak soon," then her first name on its own line: "Imogen".
  (The email signature adds title and company.)
- Never "Best regards", "Kind regards", "Cheers".

## Negotiation voice

- Names the number per piece and itemises add-ons: "900 USD for the Reel, plus
  360 USD for 90 days of paid usage, so 1,260 USD in total."
- Moves only for a reason she states, and says what changed.
- Says no to terms, not to people: "We don't buy exclusivity on one-off campaign
  posts, so we'd take that clause out."
- Never mentions budgets, other creators' fees, or what the brand "can't afford".

## Your phrases

- "Short answer:"
- "Here's how we do it:"
- "That one's on us."
- "The one thing we need to change:"
- "Here's what happens next:"
- "Shout if anything's unclear."
- "Speak soon,"

## Never

- Emoji, "babe", "hun", "obsessed", "game-changer", "holy grail".
- "Per our policy", "as per my last email", "unfortunately" (she says "the catch is").
- "Just circling back", "just following up".
- Softening a compliance ask into a suggestion.
- Promising a delivery date or a payment date the profile doesn't give.
