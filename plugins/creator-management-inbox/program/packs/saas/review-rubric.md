# Pack rubric: saas - the software-tutorial gates

Loads in Block 2 with `content-reviewer` when `business: saas`. These gates depend on what
the business sells; the structural gates (native fit, hook, CTA presence, captions,
coherence, brand, claims) stay in the reviewer. Most creators here also earn affiliate
commission, so a video that converts pays them too - say so when it helps.

## Hook (Gate 2)

- **Time / effort / money**: at least one must be prominent somewhere in the video;
  all three is ideal (spread across the video or in one sentence). Ease of use
  (effort) should virtually always be present if it is part of `{{VALUE_PROP}}` -
  in most tool categories it is the core promise.
- YouTube integrations: the "hook" is the integration's opening line - it must enter
  organically with disclosure woven in, never as a hard sponsor segue.

## Body: a tutorial with a differentiator (Gate 3)

The body is a step-by-step tutorial judged through the eyes of someone who has NEVER
heard of `{{PRODUCT}}`. If the company's priority is capturing new users, the
beginner lens applies always (advanced-user videos are a rare, explicit exception).
Check that priority against `company.md` - if the company is optimising for
expansion within an existing base instead, the lens inverts and this gate gets read
against the advanced user.

- Fewest possible steps while staying detailed enough to follow. Simple beats clever.
- **The differentiator test:** the body must answer the viewer's silent question -
  "why `{{PRODUCT}}` when I already have `{{COMPETITOR_PRIMARY}}` / [tool]?" A clear
  differentiator for THIS audience is mandatory. A generic capability that every tool
  in the category ships is not a differentiator. Even a 30-second video showcasing
  exactly one feature must pick a feature that genuinely sets the product apart.
- USP selection must fit the audience identified in Step 2. Pull the current
  priorities from `company.md` rather than hard-coding them here -
  this list changes every quarter, and a stale USP list is worse than none. The two
  buckets to distinguish:
  - **Campaign features:** whatever is currently being launched. Campaign submissions
    must actually DEMONSTRATE the campaign feature on screen, not just name-drop it.
  - **Evergreen highlights:** the durable members of `{{CORE_FEATURES}}` - the ones
    that were true last year and will be true next year. Licensing terms, asset
    breadth, and export rights belong here and are disproportionately relevant to
    business-owner audiences (`{{ICP2}}`).
- **Language rules:** use official feature names so viewers learn what things are
  called. Use the ICP's own day-to-day vocabulary - talking their language creates
  belonging. But translate internal or technical jargon (use a simpler synonym or a
  plain explanation). Advanced things are welcome; it is how they are talked about
  that must stay simple. The "always say / never say" list in `company.md` governs
  here.
- **Fluency check:** does the creator visibly know the tool? Hunting for buttons,
  wrong paths, hesitation = they did not practice before filming. NEVER tell a
  creator "it is clear you do not know the product". Instead give specific workflow
  corrections ("at 1:24 the faster way is X - it will make the flow look as smooth
  as it actually is") and flag internally so the manager can offer resources or a
  walkthrough before the next video. This matters wherever "intuitive and easy to
  use" is part of `{{VALUE_PROP}}` - the video has to make the product look
  effortless.
- **The tutorial must reach a finished, impressive output on screen.** There is
  always a logical end. The finished result should land together with the CTA.

## CTA (Gate 4)

- **Where the end CTA points, by platform:**
  - Instagram / TikTok: the promo code IS the CTA. People do not click links there -
    do not accept "link in bio" or "comment X and I'll DM you" as the primary CTA.
    Pattern: "Try `{{PRODUCT}}` for free - and if you upgrade, my code
    [their promo code] gets you `{{CREATOR_PROMO_OFFER}}`." Shorter and
    punchier is better.
  - YouTube: "link in the description." (Verifying the link sits in the first three
    lines of the description happens at post review, not content review.)
- Optional:
  - Opening CTA for long-form, worth testing: "if you're watching this on your TV,
    grab your laptop and sign up so you can follow along with me"
- Conversion ladder awareness: the baseline ask is the free signup; content that
  credibly nudges toward paid, annual, or `{{PLAN_TOP}}` is a plus. Many users start
  on a monthly `{{PLAN_MID}}` just to test and upgrade later - if the content can
  plant `{{PLAN_TOP}}` early and naturally, best. Never force it where it breaks
  native feel.

## "It must be obviously us" (Gate 6)

- **The product must be visually identifiable.** Tools in the same category look
  alike; viewers literally ask "what tool is this?" in the comments and other
  commenters answer with a competitor's name. Look for anchors:
  `{{COMPANY_DOMAIN}}` typed or visible in the URL / search bar, the logo on screen,
  the name spoken and shown. If the UI is cropped so tightly the platform is
  ambiguous, request a fix. Cropping parts of the UI is sometimes fine - ambiguity
  about which tool it is never is.

## Pricing mentions (Gate 7)

- Never mandatory, but organic price mentions are usually in our benefit - they counter
  the "all these capabilities must be expensive" assumption. Fact-check against
  `{{COMPANY_DOMAIN}}/pricing` too.

## Format and length norms

- **Short-form (IG + TikTok, one review covers both):** minimum 30 seconds; sweet
  spot 30-45s (45 is best); up to ~3 minutes is fine. No hard maximum - the real
  bar is "interesting the whole way through, with everything needed to spark
  checking the product out". 30 seconds is enough for exactly one feature - and then
  that feature must be a true differentiator.
- **YouTube integration:** the length agreed with the creator (brief or `deals.md`) is
  a contract term - verify the runtime, including mid-video. None agreed = no minimum
  to enforce (`no-invented-spec`).
- **Dedicated YouTube video:** at least 6 minutes; sweet spot 7-12 minutes, going
  genuinely deep on a workflow or feature.
- **Carousels / statics (a small minority of content):** single-frame statics almost
  never work - question the format choice. For carousels, check the format can still
  carry all three pillars: slide 1 = hook (pain or wow), middle slides = the
  education, final slide = CTA. Logo, brand, and "obviously us" checks still apply.

## Titles (the go-live reply)

**Title advice is a recommendation, not a menu.** End-product-first titles -
what the audience walks away with, never the tool or the process. Mark a
favourite ("← my fav"). Work the product-tutorial phrasing in as the SEO
keyword variant. Keep positioning consistent with `company.md` - never
describe the product in a way the brand does not.
For answer-engine optimisation, the citable one-line answer goes "in the first
5 lines of the description".
