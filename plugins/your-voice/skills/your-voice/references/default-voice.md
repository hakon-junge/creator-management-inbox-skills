# Default voice - used until you build your own

> Direct, warm and clean: the point first, one clear ask, nothing said twice. Built
> from principles, not from anyone's catchphrases. Say "build my voice" to replace it
> with your own, learned from your sent writing.

## Core voice

- **Direct.** The point goes in the first two lines, stated with confidence. No
  warm-up and no hedging ("just", "I think maybe", "sort of") unless the doubt is
  real. Busy readers decide from the top and skim the rest.
  "We can do two videos in March. Here's the plan."
- **Warm, not soft.** Warmth comes from noticing something specific and saying so.
  Enthusiasm only when it's earned, and then concrete. Generic praise reads as a
  template; specific praise reads as attention.
  "The cold open with the empty calendar is a great hook." Not: "Love your content!"
- **Straight about gaps.** When budget, timing or fit doesn't work, say so plainly
  and show the reasoning or the numbers. Never apologise for a price or a budget: a
  clear reason is respected, an apology invites haggling.
  "That's above our budget for a first video. We base fees on average views, which
  puts your channel at [amount]."
- **Low-pressure asks.** One ask, framed as an invitation, with an easy way to say
  no. People agree more readily when no is allowed, and a no that costs nothing
  keeps the door open.
  "Would Thursday work? If not, send me a day that does."
- **Say it once.** Each point appears once: no recap, no "as mentioned above", no
  second reason when the first one carries it. Repetition reads as nervousness and
  buries the ask.
  "The fee covers one video and 30 days of usage." Not followed by: "So, to sum
  up: one video, 30 days."
- **Credit by name, for something specific.** Thank people for what they actually
  did. Specific credit is what makes people want to work with you again.
  "Thanks to [Name] for turning the numbers around overnight - it made this an easy
  decision."

## Openers and greetings

- **First message to someone new:** "Hi [Name]," then one line on why you're writing
  that could only be about them: "Your video on budgeting apps is the clearest
  walkthrough I've seen this year."
- **Reply:** "Hi [Name]," then the answer, or a specific thanks if they earned one:
  "Thanks for sending the numbers so quickly."
- **Quick mid-thread answer:** no greeting. The answer is the first word: "Yes,
  Tuesday works."
- **Stakes set the formality:** "Hello [Name]," for a first formal contact or a senior
  stranger; "Hey [Name]," once they write that way to you. Mirror informality, never
  lead it.
- The first line always carries information. If warmth helps, make it specific
  ("Congrats on the launch last week"), not filler.

## Closers

- **After an ask:** the next step, specific. "If Thursday works, I'll send the brief
  the same day."
- **After good news or a done deal:** one forward line. "Excited to see the first cut."
- **After a no:** the door where it really is. "If your schedule opens up in spring,
  I'd be glad to pick this up again."
- **Quick replies:** no closer.
- The signature carries the name, so don't type it above. A sign-off word ("Thanks,"
  or "Best,") is optional and never stacked on a second closing line.

## Registers

- **Email** (partners, clients, creators): greeting matched to the stakes, the point
  in the first two lines, one clear ask, a specific close. Most replies fit in 3-8
  short lines. Mid-thread quick replies drop the greeting and the closer.
- **Chat** (Slack, Teams): short, line breaks instead of paragraphs, no greeting.
  When something needs a decision, state it, give the options, recommend one:
  "Need a call on the March slot:
  1. Move to April - safer, but we lose the launch tie-in
  2. Keep March with one video instead of two
  I'd go with 2. OK?"
- **LinkedIn:** editorial and personal. Open on a relatable moment or a tension,
  build to the insight, end with a genuine question. A short list, or an arrow (↓)
  leading into one, is fine. No corporate speak, no announcement dressed as a story.
  Opens like: "Last quarter a creator turned down our offer. It was the most useful
  no of the year." Ends like: "What's the best feedback you've had from a no?"
- **Docs:** the goal in one line, then the context the reader needs, then numbered
  points, then outcomes and next steps, each with an owner and a date.

## Shapes

Principles, not scripts. The facts come from the task.

- **First reply to an interested contact.** Thank them for one specific thing, say
  what you're proposing in a line or two, and ask for the one thing you need next
  (their numbers, a date, a yes). Save the details for the next message.
- **Feedback on someone's work.** Specific praise first: what works and why. Then any
  required change, stated plainly. Then optional ideas, marked optional. Hand the
  decision back: "You know your audience best."
- **A decline.** The reason is fit or timing, never the quality of their work. Offer
  a real door if one exists, and none if it doesn't. Stay in writing: a call offered
  to soften a no reads as a consolation prize.
- **A number or a negotiation.** The number travels with how it was reached ("based
  on average views over your last ten videos"). One clear offer, not a range to be
  bargained down. An easy exit: "If that doesn't work on your side, I understand."
- **A nudge or follow-up.** Restate the ask, not the offer: "Does Thu 12 March still
  work for you?" One nudge, then let it rest; a second chase costs more goodwill
  than the answer is worth.

## Formatting

- Short paragraphs of 1-3 sentences, with a blank line between them.
- Bullets for 3 or more parallel items; two items stay in a sentence.
- Bold only for the one thing to act on (a date, a decision), once per message at most.
- No em dashes. A spaced hyphen ( - ) instead.
- Emoji: none in work email, at most one elsewhere. Your own samples can turn them on.
- Exclamation marks: sparingly, one per message at most, never in a decline or next
  to a number.
- Links: descriptive words for anything to click ("the brief"); a bare URL on its own
  line for anything to copy.
- Numbers: the unit or currency next to the figure ("800 USD per video"); dates with
  the weekday when scheduling ("Thu 12 March").

## Phrases

None, on purpose: a default voice borrows nobody's catchphrases. Yours are added when
you build your voice.

## Never

- Stiff openers: "I hope this email finds you well", "As per my last email",
  "Kindly be advised". Start with the point instead.
- A bare, generic closer ("Looking forward to hearing from you!") where a specific
  forward line fits.
- "Honest" as a frame: "to be honest", "I'll be honest". It implies the rest wasn't.
  Say direct, straight or transparent: "To be direct, ..."
- Restating a point already made, or summarising the message at its end.
- Over-explaining: background the reader already has, a second and third reason, the
  history of a decision.
- Corporate filler: "circle back", "touch base", "leverage", "synergy", "at your
  earliest convenience", "please do not hesitate to contact me".
- "Just checking in" or "just following up" with nothing after it.
- Apologising for a price, a budget or a no.
